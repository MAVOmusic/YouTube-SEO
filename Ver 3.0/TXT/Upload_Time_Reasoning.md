# Why these six times

Chosen 10 November 2026 from the channel's own Insights screenshots of that day, not from a generic best-time article. The six are 02:00, 05:00, 13:30, 16:00, 19:00 and 23:00 Budapest, and the long form premieres at the slot time with the Short five minutes later.

## The two things the data actually shows

1. **The viewing heatmap has two bright bands.** Set to the channel's local time, GMT plus 1, the last 28 days are brightest roughly 12:00 to 14:00 and again roughly 18:00 to 20:00. The window from about 02:00 to 08:00 is the darkest part of the week. Saturday is the brightest column overall and weekends beat weekdays at every hour.
2. **The audience is one fifth American, one tenth British.** United States 18.5%, United Kingdom 10.4%, Japan 4.6%, Indonesia 2.5%, Germany 2.5%. Mobile carries 58.3% of watch time and computer 30.6%, which means most viewing is personal-device viewing rather than a shared living-room screen.

A single time cannot serve both of those. Budapest noon is midnight in Los Angeles. So the six slots are split into two heatmap slots, three geography slots and one edge slot, and the split is stated openly rather than hidden behind "best time to post".

## Why these are not arbitrary, and what they still are not

- Every slot maps to a named band on the heatmap or a named country in the geography list with its share. None of the six is chosen because it is round, convenient, or common in articles.
- The two slots with the most audience behind them are 13:30 and 19:00, and they are the two that sit on the heatmap peaks. If a day has one song expected to do better than the rest, it belongs there.
- **Caveat that matters:** YouTube states that publish time is not known to directly affect long-term performance, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en, and Studio repeats it on the heatmap card. So these slots are for premiere attendance, for a stable comparison window, and for spreading six releases across the day. They are not a claim that a song posted at 13:30 will outperform the same song at 16:00.
- The heatmap measures the whole channel, not one song. It is 94% new viewers, so it reflects the Shorts feed more than it reflects the long-form audience.
- Only 0.4% of subscribers have all notifications on and 0.2% have all plus personalised, so the premiere is the mechanism that can actually pull people in at a chosen hour. That is the reason the premiere time gets the heatmap peaks rather than a random slot.
- Any slot that stops earning its place gets dropped. The right way to test that is the 28-day view, not one upload.

## What would change the schedule

- A country moving above 10% in the geography list.
- The heatmap bands shifting by two hours or more across two consecutive reads.
- US share falling below about 12%, which would make the 02:00 and 05:00 slots hard to justify.
- A run of premieres showing materially different live attendance for one slot against another at matched song strength.
