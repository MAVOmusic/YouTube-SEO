# THE RIGHTS TEST - RUN THIS BEFORE RECORDING ANYTHING NEW
################################################################################

  The claim does not follow the song. It follows the RECORDING and the
  COMPOSITION as registered. So the question is never "is this song famous",
  it is "has anyone registered this specific recording".

  Score a candidate against these. Two or more YES is a live candidate.

    [ ] 1. Is the composition public domain? (composer dead 70+ years, or
           traditional/folk). Chopin cleared on this alone.
    [ ] 2. Was the track ever released as a single?  NO is good.
    [ ] 3. Was it a bonus track, a b-side, a deluxe extra, a regional-only
           release, or a soundtrack throwaway?  YES is good. This is the
           CO-0022 profile.
    [ ] 4. Does it appear on a greatest-hits or compilation record?  NO is good.
           Compilations mean active catalogue management.
    [ ] 5. Is the rights holder a major or a large independent with a
           catalogue team?  NO is good.
    [ ] 6. Does the song have a large existing cover/remix ecosystem on
           YouTube?  NO is good. Heavy cover traffic means active enforcement.
    [ ] 7. Can you find it in YouTube Content ID's own claimed-works data, or
           does every existing upload of it show ads and no "Ineligible"
           badge?  YES is good.

  TWO KNOWN COUNTER-EXAMPLES. BOTH FAILED. READ BOTH BEFORE BUILDING.

  COUNTER-EXAMPLE 1 - the source being obscure protects nothing.
  CO-0023 Sabaton - Camouflage is a cover of an OBSCURE 1986 Stan Ridgway
  one-hit-wonder, and it is E4 INELIGIBLE. "The original is obscure" is NOT a
  shortcut. Nuclear Blast registered the Sabaton recording because Sabaton is a
  large act. Obscurity of the SOURCE means nothing if the PERFORMER is big.

  COUNTER-EXAMPLE 2 - being a deep cut protects nothing either, and this one
  directly damages the Tier B premise. Added 02-11-2026.
  CO-0024 Sabaton - The Hammer Has Fallen is the textbook Tier B candidate:
  never a single, never on a studio album proper, only ever released on a 2001
  demo compilation and a 2007 reissue of the same recordings. By every rule in
  the list above it should be unclaimed. It is E4 INELIGIBLE, GBP 0.00.
  Nuclear Blast registered the Metalizer reissue as a unit, so the album track
  inherited a claim it would never have earned on its own.

  WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE TEST
  Registration is not predictable from fame, label size or obscurity. It is an
  empirical fact about each individual recording, and the only way to know it in
  advance is to look:

      BEFORE BUILDING ANYTHING, open YouTube and find three existing uploads of
      the exact same song. Then ask:
        - Are the label's own uploads monetised and claimed?
        - Do other people's covers of it show ads, or a claim notice?
        - Does the song appear in Content ID claim lists at all?
      If the answer to the first is yes and the second shows claims, DO NOT
      BUILD. If uploads exist with no claim and no ads beyond the uploader's own,
      you have a live candidate.

  Scoring the seven points gives you a candidate. This check gives you a verdict.
  Only the verdict counts.

  *** CRITICAL CORRECTION TO TIER A - ADDED 02-11-2026 (CO-0025) ***
  Tier A says public-domain compositions carry zero claim risk. That is TRUE ONLY
  WHEN THE ARRANGEMENT IS MAVO'S OWN.

  CO-0025 Aephanemer - Old French Song is a cover of TCHAIKOVSKY, who died in
  1893 and whose Op. 39 No. 16 is unambiguously public domain. It is E4
  INELIGIBLE, GBP 0.00, because MAVO played AEPHANEMER'S ARRANGEMENT of it, and
  Aephanemer's recording is registered to Napalm Records.

  The composition being free does not free the recording. Two different rights
  exist on every cover:
      1. the COMPOSITION (publishing) - Tchaikovsky's, long expired, free
      2. the RECORDING and ARRANGEMENT - Napalm's, live, claimed

  CO-0011 Chopin works because MAVO built his own four-hour arrangement of a
  public-domain piece. CO-0025 fails because he copied a 2021 metal band's
  arrangement of a public-domain piece. Same public-domain status, opposite
  outcome.

  TIER A, RESTATED: public domain means you may ARRANGE IT YOURSELF. It never
  means you may COVER A MODERN RECORDING OF IT. Covering a metal band's Bach or
  Beethoven or Tchaikovsky arrangement is a Tier B risk with a classical
  disguise, and it fails the same way.

  BEST CURRENT HYPOTHESIS, UNPROVEN
  Claim coverage follows REISSUE and PROMOTION, not catalogue size. A track that
  was swept into a remaster or reissue gets registered with everything else on
  it. A track nobody ever reissued or promoted can fall through the same label's
  net - which is the CO-0022 shape. Under this hypothesis, the newest recordings
  and the most recently reissued ones are the WORST candidates, and the quiet
  30-year-old bonus tracks are the best.

    This is the only tier where eligibility can be guaranteed before recording.

    in Tier B, not Tier A, and you will fail exactly like CO-0025 did. **

  TIER B - deep cuts and bonus tracks from claimed artists.
    Same artist, same catalogue, wrong track to police. CO-0022 is the proof.
    Look for: deluxe/bonus/b-side, never a single, absent from greatest hits.
    ** WARNING - THIS TIER ALREADY HAS ONE FAILURE. ** CO-0024 (Sabaton, The
    Hammer Has Fallen) scored full marks on every stated criterion and came back
    E4. Deep-cut status is necessary but NOT sufficient. Run the three-upload
    check from the rights test on every Tier B candidate before recording.
    Do not batch-build Tier B. Build one, wait, read the badge, then decide.

    Unverified as a tier. No corpus evidence yet. Test with one upload.

  DO NOT BUILD - anything whose full catalogue is actively enforced:

    Every one of these is already in the register at E4 and will stay there.
