## DESCRIPTION (1538 characters)

```text
It is the last song on the record and it is not the twelfth chapter of anything. Hoppus took the name from chapter thirteen of Dante's Inferno, the one about people who take their own lives, and then wrote a song about a man who sees a woman across a crowded room and knows exactly how it ends. He has described that image himself. It is a strange note to finish a debut album on, which is exactly why it works.

The shape of the track is the point. Chapter XIII runs over five minutes, which is enormous for this band, and the last two of those minutes are an outro that refuses to resolve. The whole album is built on Jerry Finn's clean separation between the parts, and this closer is where that pays off: the record ends by removing things rather than adding them.

On bass, that demands patience. The part barely moves for four minutes, then the outro asks you to sit on the same figure while the guitars get louder around you. The temptation is to fill. Do not. Almost every good version of this song is somebody doing less than they want to.

Thirteen November 2006, Interscope, five minutes seven on the record. This play along runs four minutes fifty.
```
