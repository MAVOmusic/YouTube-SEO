Play this one loud enough and the choir does the rest. The Lion From The North is the second track on Carolus Rex, four and a half minutes of march tempo, massed voices and a hook that is Latin rather than English, recorded at The Abyss in Sweden with Peter Tägtgren and mixed so the room is part of the arrangement.

The subject is Gustavus Adolphus, the king Sweden called the lion of the north, who took a small country into the middle of Europe and won until he did not. Brodén does not write about him from a distance: the chorus is a shout, the verses are dispatches, and the keyboards carry a tune a field band could have played in 1632.

For the bass this is a stamina exercise disguised as a simple one. The figure repeats, the tempo does not flatter you, and the job is to keep the pulse even while guitars and voices stack up above it. Do not decorate it. Hold the line.

It has stayed in the live set for more than a decade, which tells you what the band thinks of it. The Carolus Rex material is the part of the catalogue they lean on hardest in a room full of people, and this is one of the reasons why: the crowd already knows where the shout goes.

Twenty five May 2012, Nuclear Blast, four minutes forty two on the record.
