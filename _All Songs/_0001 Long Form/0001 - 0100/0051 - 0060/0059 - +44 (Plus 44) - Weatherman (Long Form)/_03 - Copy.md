## DESCRIPTION (1511 characters)

```text
Four minutes thirty three, and almost none of it is loud. Weatherman does not build to a release, it narrows: the guitars come back, the vocal stays dry and close, and the chorus refuses the lift the rest of the album keeps promising. It is the least comfortable thing on When Your Heart Stops Beating.

Hoppus and Barker wrote it in the first months after blink-182 stopped, and it carries that. No jokes, no relief, no big finish, and an interlude placed right before it to clear the ground. What is left is a slow, cold track about somebody who will not say what is wrong.

The bass has the hardest job on the record here. With nothing to hide behind, tone and timing are the whole performance: long notes, small movements, and a chorus that asks you to stay exactly where you are while everything above shifts.

There is no line here to quote at somebody and no moment that works out of context, which is why it never turns up in the same conversation as the singles. It is the song on the album that rewards the listener who stayed, and it punishes the one who skipped to track ten.

Thirteen November 2006, Interscope, four minutes thirty three on the record.
```
