## DESCRIPTION (1584 characters)

```text
It starts in a basement and never quite leaves it. Little Death is the +44 song that kept being taken apart: Hoppus and Barker began working on it down in Travis's home studio before the album had a name, and it went through more versions than anything else they recorded. What survived is the slowest, murkiest thing on the record.

That texture is the arrangement's whole argument. Where the rest of When Your Heart Stops Beating pushes the guitars forward, this one holds them back and lets the low end carry the weight, which is why four minutes of it feels bigger than its length suggests.

Playing it is a test of patience. The line is long, mostly on the root, and the moment you add a fill the mood goes with it. Let the notes ring, sit a little behind the beat, and let the room do the work.

There are two ways to record a song like this and the band chose the harder one. A version with a big chorus and a wall of guitars would have been easy to place on the album and easy to forget. This one leaves gaps where a hook should be and trusts the emptiness to carry the weight instead.

Thirteen November 2006, Interscope, four minutes five on the record and four minutes here.
```
