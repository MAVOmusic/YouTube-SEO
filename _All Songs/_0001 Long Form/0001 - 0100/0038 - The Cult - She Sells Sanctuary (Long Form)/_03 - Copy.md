## DESCRIPTION (1635 characters)

```text
There is a guitar figure running through this that never resolves. It circles the same three chords for four minutes and the song gets its entire power from the fact that it refuses to land - which is a strange thing to engineer on purpose, and it was.

The Cult wanted Steve Lillywhite for Love. They got Steve Brown instead, a producer best known at that point for Wham!, and the band were somewhere between irritated and amused about it. What Brown understood was that Billy Duffy's riff did not need a chorus to release it. Sanctuary came out as a single in May 1985, months before the album, and it has been in films and adverts ever since precisely because it hypnotises rather than climaxes.

Jamie Stewart played the bass, the keyboards and the strings on this record. That explains the synth humming under the bass, and the reason the arrangement sounds like one person thinking rather than a band negotiating: the line is doubling a ghost. It also means the line is simple by necessity: it is holding a groove that three other instruments are borrowing from.

Four minutes eight here, which is the 12 inch length. The single edit is nearly two minutes shorter and loses the point entirely.
```
